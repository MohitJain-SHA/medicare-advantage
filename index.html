If you're on Medicare, you've probably noticed that the choices don't stay put. Plan costs, doctor networks, and drug coverage are reset every year, and a plan that fit well last year may not be the best fit for next year.

What Medicare Advantage is

Medicare Advantage (also called Part C) is a way to get your Medicare benefits through a private insurance company that contracts with Medicare. These plans include Part A (hospital) and Part B (medical) coverage. Many also include Part D prescription drug coverage, and some add benefits Original Medicare doesn't, like dental, vision, or hearing. What's included depends on the plan.

What's worth comparing

  • Your doctors and hospitals. Most plans use a network, so it matters whether the providers you see are in it.
  • Your prescriptions. Each plan has its own drug list and cost tiers, so the same medication can cost different amounts under different plans.
  • What you'd pay. Monthly premium, deductible, copays, and the yearly out-of-pocket maximum.
  • Extra benefits. Dental, vision, hearing, and other extras vary by plan and by county.

When you can make a change

You can't switch plans at any time. Most changes happen during set enrollment periods, so it helps to know which one applies to you.

Oct 15 – Dec 7
Annual Enrollment Period. Join, switch, or drop a Medicare Advantage or drug plan. Changes start January 1.
Jan 1 – Mar 31
Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment. For people already in a Medicare Advantage plan who want to switch to another plan or return to Original Medicare.
Around your 65th birthday
Initial Enrollment Period. A seven-month window that starts three months before the month you turn 65.

Some people also qualify for a Special Enrollment Period, for example after a move or a change in other coverage. A licensed agent can tell you which window applies to you.
